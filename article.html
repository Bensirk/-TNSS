The Culture Doesn't Need Your Permission.

The best culture has never waited for approval. So why should it start now?

JE
Jordan Ellis
August 27, 2026 · 7 min read

Culture has never been particularly interested in behaving itself. The moments that change music, film, nightlife and art rarely begin with everyone agreeing that something is a good idea.

They usually begin with somebody doing something that makes other people uncomfortable.

The problem with playing it safe

Entertainment has always been a conversation between creators and audiences. But somewhere along the way, the conversation became a calculation.

What will perform? What will trend? What will generate engagement? What won't offend anyone?

Those questions may make sense for a corporation. They don't necessarily make sense for culture.

The safest idea is rarely the most interesting one.

This is why the stories worth remembering are often the ones that challenge the audience instead of reassuring it.

Make something worth arguing about

A good cultural publication shouldn't tell readers what to think. It should give them something worth thinking about.

Sometimes that means celebrating something brilliant. Sometimes it means asking why everyone else thinks something is brilliant.

Either way, disagreement isn't a failure.
